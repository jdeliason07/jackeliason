Simplicity is not the absence of things. It is the presence of only what is needed, arranged so that nothing competes with the point. A sentence is simple when every word carries weight; a page is simple when the eye knows where to go.

## Subtraction

Antoine de Saint-Exupéry wrote that perfection is reached not when there is nothing left to add, but when there is nothing left to take away.[^1] The work is mostly *removal*: of the clever phrase, of the second example, of the paragraph written to prove effort rather than to say something.

> Our life is frittered away by detail. … Simplicity, simplicity, simplicity!

That is Thoreau, from *Walden*.[^2] A blockquote is set with a single rule and the same type as the body.

## Lists
